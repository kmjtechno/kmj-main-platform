# KMJ Public Experience — Locked Direction

**Status:** OWNER LOCKED  
**Scope:** KMJ Main Platform landing page, public product pages, product artwork, demos, SEO/AEO presentation and future public-facing additions.

This file is an authoritative product/design constraint. Future chats, developers, agents and automation must preserve it unless the owner explicitly changes the lock.

## 1. Experience objective

The public experience must feel premium, cinematic, modern, cooperative, professional and trustworthy. The first impression should be visually magnetic and memorable without becoming noisy, gimmicky, gaming-like or difficult to use.

Use strong visual hierarchy and intentional eye travel: focal imagery -> product meaning -> benefit -> proof/status -> next action.

## 2. Colour is semantic, not decoration

Do **not** make the whole site red.

- KMJ red: brand signature, primary actions and selective emphasis.
- Blue/cyan: cloud, connectivity, infrastructure and technical systems where meaningful.
- Green: verified/available/success states.
- Amber/orange: preview, early access, attention or transitional states.
- Neutral black/charcoal/white: enterprise foundation, readability and trust.
- Other colours may be used when they improve the meaning of a product, industry or scene.

Maintain visual cohesion through typography, spacing, surfaces, lighting and interaction language rather than forcing one accent colour everywhere.

## 3. Product artwork

Every public product must have its **own separate, unique product-specific artwork**. Do not use one combined collage as the product asset and do not substitute generic placeholder art.

Artwork should explain the product visually at a glance. Keep the premium cinematic quality while making products visibly distinct. In particular, KMJ 360 Dome and KMJ Cinema Player must never look like the same product.

Default artwork target: 4:3 master with intentional desktop/mobile crops. Optimize delivery for responsive loading and performance.

Do not place decorative KMJ/KMJ TECHNO logo watermarks over artwork. Product/device branding may appear only when it belongs naturally to the depicted product/interface.

## 4. Product communication

A customer should quickly understand:
1. What is this?
2. Who is it for?
3. What problem does it solve?
4. What concrete benefit does it provide?
5. What is its current lifecycle/availability?
6. Can I see or try it?
7. What is the appropriate next action?

Product copy should be persuasive, clear and commercially useful, but must remain factual. Never fabricate capabilities, customers, certifications, benchmarks, market leadership, availability or proof.

Do not publish unsupported claims such as "#1", "world's best", guaranteed results or guaranteed search rankings. The quality target may be world-class; public claims require evidence.

## 5. Interactive understanding

Where interaction materially helps understanding, provide a lightweight browser demo/simulation. Demos must be clearly labelled as simulations when they are not connected to a real backend/product environment.

Prioritize demos for products whose workflow or value is difficult to communicate with static copy alone. Demo UX must explain the workflow, not merely animate the page.

## 6. Search and AI discovery

Optimize each canonical product page for conventional search engines and AI answer/search systems through:
- unique intent-aligned titles and descriptions;
- crawlable factual product copy;
- clear headings and semantic page structure;
- Product/SoftwareApplication or other appropriate structured data where factually valid;
- FAQ content when it answers real customer questions;
- use cases and audience context;
- canonical URLs;
- strong internal linking from landing, portfolio and related products;
- descriptive image alt text and optimized media;
- consistent product names, lifecycle facts and terminology across surfaces.

Do not keyword-stuff, generate doorway pages, hide text or use deceptive SEO. Search ranking is an outcome, not a guaranteed claim.

## 7. Conversion without deception

Design the path from curiosity -> understanding -> confidence -> demo/trial/contact/purchase. Calls to action must match actual lifecycle and commercial readiness.

Purchase stays fail-closed unless governed product state allows it. Research/planned products must not be presented as currently purchasable. Early access, preview and trial labels must reflect actual platform state.

## 8. Performance and accessibility

Visual richness must not sacrifice speed, mobile usability, accessibility or stability. Use responsive media, lazy loading below the fold, appropriate eager/preload only for critical media, readable contrast, keyboard-accessible interactions and reduced-motion-friendly behavior.

Desktop and mobile are first-class experiences.

## 9. Landing-page role

The landing page should orchestrate the portfolio rather than repeat every product page. It must create a compelling first impression, surface flagship products, show the wider ecosystem, communicate industries/use cases and roadmap truth, and route users to canonical product pages.

Preserve the owner-locked hero copy and established brand identity unless explicitly changed by the owner.

## 10. Change-control rule

Before merging a public-experience change, verify:
- it follows this specification;
- product facts/lifecycle are truthful;
- artwork is product-specific;
- desktop and mobile rendering are healthy;
- no unexpected horizontal overflow or console/runtime errors;
- canonical product links work;
- relevant CI/tests pass;
- production is not deployed merely because a design change merged.

When visual preference conflicts with factual accuracy, security, accessibility, lifecycle governance or purchase controls, those safety/truth constraints win.
## 11. P0 world-class quality principle

This is a **P0 project priority**, not a cosmetic preference.

Every public-experience decision must be evaluated holistically across visual design, UX, product comprehension, factual accuracy, conversion, SEO/AEO discoverability, interactive demonstration, accessibility, responsive behavior, performance, security, maintainability and operational governance.

Do not optimize one dimension by damaging another. A visually impressive page that is slow, confusing, inaccessible, misleading or difficult to maintain does not meet the bar. Likewise, a technically correct page that feels generic, forgettable or commercially unclear does not meet the bar.

For each meaningful landing/product change, actively ask:
- Is the purpose immediately understandable?
- Is the visual hierarchy compelling and intentional from first glance through the next action?
- Are colour, imagery, motion and interaction meaningful rather than decorative noise?
- Does the experience feel mature, cooperative, professional and trustworthy?
- Are claims, lifecycle, pricing/access and demos truthful?
- Is the content useful to both humans and search/AI systems without keyword stuffing?
- Is there a clear conversion path appropriate to the product state?
- Does it work exceptionally well on mobile and desktop?
- Is it fast, accessible, secure and maintainable?
- Is this materially better than a generic template or ordinary product page?

The target is world-class execution quality. Never publish unsupported superiority claims merely to express that internal quality target.

## 12. Interactive product showroom standard

Treat the public platform as an interactive product showroom, not a conventional brochure website.

### Ten-second comprehension
Within roughly ten seconds on a product's first screen, a new visitor should be able to understand what the product is, who it serves, the primary problem/outcome, its current lifecycle state and the appropriate next action.

### Product-specific visual storytelling
Allow each product family to have meaningful visual atmosphere while preserving KMJ cohesion. Cloud/infrastructure may use cooler technical tones; calibration may use optical/precision cues; immersive cinema may use richer cinematic spectrum; security/licensing may use restrained enterprise cues. Colour must communicate, not merely decorate.

### Interactive demos as explanation
When a workflow benefits from interaction, make a lightweight interactive demo/simulation a primary explanation surface. Candidate experiences include calibration mapping/warping, CloudOS infrastructure control, remote-session flows, licensing activation/entitlements and cinema playback/show control. Simulations must be explicitly identified when they are not a real connected product environment.

### Contextual Kristi guidance
Kristi should behave as a contextual product guide rather than a generic chat bubble. On a product surface, guidance should be grounded in verified facts for that product and help visitors understand use cases, lifecycle, demos and appropriate next steps. Kristi must not invent capabilities, availability, pricing or proof.

### Evidence-first trust
Pair persuasive communication with verifiable product facts, lifecycle state, documentation/demo evidence and relevant security/architecture information. Do not fabricate customer counts, deployments, testimonials, awards, benchmarks, uptime/reliability percentages or other social proof.

### Intent-based discovery
In addition to product-name navigation, provide paths based on customer intent where useful: cloud infrastructure, immersive experiences, remote access, AI engineering, calibration, licensing and other evidence-backed solution groups.

### Search/AI entity architecture
Each product should have one canonical entity page with consistent naming and facts, appropriate structured data, crawlable use cases/FAQs, meaningful related-product links, sitemap discoverability and terminology consistent with the governed catalog.

### Performance budget
Cinematic presentation is subject to hard performance discipline. Prefer responsive AVIF/WebP where supported, correctly sized sources, critical-media prioritization, below-fold lazy loading, restrained animation and reduced-motion support. Core Web Vitals regressions must be treated as product defects, not accepted as a visual-design cost.

### Lifecycle-specific conversion
Calls to action must follow governed state:
- Available: eligible purchase/trial/demo/contact actions only when actually supported.
- Preview/Early Access/Public Beta: appropriate access/request/demo actions.
- Research/Planned: roadmap, learn-more or interest actions; never imply immediate purchase.
Purchase remains fail-closed under platform authority.

### Signature KMJ moment
Create a distinctive, performant hero/early-page interaction that communicates the connected KMJ ecosystem rather than looking like a generic template. A preferred concept is a controlled visual transition connecting Cloud -> Calibration -> Immersive Cinema -> AI/Remote systems as one governed ecosystem. It must remain usable, accessible and reduced-motion friendly.

### No fabricated proof
Concept artwork may contain illustrative visual placeholders during exploration, but production UI must not publish invented testimonials, customer identities, global deployment claims, reliability percentages, awards or numerical proof. Only evidence-backed proof may ship.

## 13. Signature KMJ ecosystem experience — implementation contract

### Hero interaction
Preserve the owner-locked headline: **Technology engineered to connect complex systems.**

The hero's signature interaction is a connected ecosystem constellation with four truthful domain nodes:
1. **Cloud** — infrastructure and CloudOS.
2. **Calibration** — precision mapping and Calibration Pro.
3. **Immersive** — cinema, dome and playback systems.
4. **AI + Remote** — engineering automation, secure remote/control products.

The interaction must communicate that these are distinct products/domains connected through the governed KMJ customer platform. It must not imply that unfinished products are generally available.

On capable pointers, focus/hover may emphasize one domain and reveal a concise factual outcome plus canonical product route. Keyboard focus must provide the same information. On touch/mobile, the nodes remain directly tappable and readable without requiring hover.

Use restrained CSS-first motion: slow ambient orbit/connection energy, small depth/parallax only where inexpensive, and short state transitions. Avoid scroll-jacking, autoplay video backgrounds, WebGL as a requirement, cursor hijacking, or continuous high-cost animation.

Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, render the complete ecosystem as a stable composition with no information loss.

### Eye-travel sequence
The intended visual reading path is:
**brand/domain eyebrow -> locked headline -> concise value proposition -> primary CTA -> ecosystem constellation -> proof/lifecycle cues -> next section**.

The visual focal point must never compete with the headline or CTA. Product meaning comes before spectacle.

### Landing narrative
The canonical section sequence is:
1. Signature hero / connected ecosystem.
2. Flagship product handoff.
3. Governed product ecosystem.
4. Immersive Cinema Suite.
5. Main Platform trust, licensing and controlled access.
6. Industries / customer-intent pathways backed by real product capabilities.
7. Truthful roadmap / lifecycle outlook.
8. Final conversion CTA and footer.

Sections may be combined when this improves mobile comprehension or performance, but the narrative order must remain recognizable.

### Section transitions
Transitions should feel like one continuous KMJ system rather than unrelated templates. Use shared grid/radius/typography/surface language plus semantic atmosphere shifts:
- Cloud/infrastructure: cool blue/cyan technical light.
- Calibration: precision optics, measured grids and controlled spectral cues.
- Immersive cinema: richer cinematic light and depth.
- AI/remote engineering: violet/cyan or other restrained computational cues.
- Licensing/security/control: neutral graphite with selective verified/status accents.
- KMJ red: brand signature and high-value action emphasis, not a blanket page colour.

Use gradients, edge light, subtle grid/line continuity and controlled background handoffs rather than hard decorative separators.

### Product handoff behavior
Each hero domain must lead to real canonical product destinations or a truthful grouped discovery surface. Never create dead visual controls. Lifecycle state remains visible when relevant, and CTA language must follow governed availability.

### Performance/accessibility gates
The signature experience must:
- remain understandable with CSS/JS motion disabled;
- preserve semantic headings and crawlable copy;
- support keyboard navigation and visible focus;
- avoid information encoded by colour alone;
- avoid horizontal overflow at 390px;
- avoid layout shift from hero media;
- keep critical hero media bounded and responsive;
- introduce no console/runtime errors;
- treat Core Web Vitals regressions as defects.

### Acceptance test
The experience passes only if a first-time visitor can answer, without scrolling deeply:
- What does KMJ TECHNO build?
- What are the major solution domains?
- How are they connected?
- Which product/path should I explore next?
- What is actually available versus preview/research/planned?

Visual magnetism is required, but comprehension, truth, performance and professional trust are hard constraints.

## 14. Signature hero — exact interaction state machine

### Shared semantic states

The hero has one semantic state model across every input mode:

- **IDLE** — all four ecosystem domains are visible with equal semantic availability; the central governed-platform connection is visible; no product is implied to be selected.
- **PREVIEW(domain)** — one domain receives visual emphasis and its short factual outcome is exposed. This is exploratory only and must not navigate.
- **FOCUS(domain)** — keyboard focus equivalent of PREVIEW. It exposes the same information and visible focus treatment.
- **SELECTED(domain)** — touch/mobile persistent disclosure state. The domain summary and canonical CTA remain open until another domain is selected, the user dismisses it, or navigation occurs.
- **NAVIGATING(domain)** — entered only after an explicit activation of the domain CTA/link. Normal browser navigation semantics apply.

Lifecycle/availability labels are data-driven and are never changed by interaction state.

### Desktop: fine pointer + hover

**Initial:** IDLE.

**Trigger to PREVIEW:** pointer enters a domain node or its associated visual region.

**Transition:** 180–240ms ease-out. Selected domain may scale no more than ~1.02, edge/connection luminance increases, supporting copy fades/translates in by no more than 6–8px, and unrelated nodes reduce emphasis slightly but remain readable. Do not blur essential text.

**Pointer movement:** no cursor-following animation and no high-frequency React state updates. Optional parallax must be CSS/compositor-friendly, bounded to a few pixels and disabled on low-motion/reduced-motion paths.

**Exit:** pointer leaving the complete interactive domain region returns to IDLE after a short 80–120ms grace period to avoid flicker while moving toward its CTA.

**Click:** clicking the actual canonical link/CTA navigates. Clicking decorative background must do nothing. If the whole node is implemented as a link, it must have one unambiguous destination and accessible name.

**No auto-selection:** ambient motion must never cycle PREVIEW between products.

### Keyboard

All four domain controls/links are reachable in logical DOM order: Cloud -> Calibration -> Immersive -> AI + Remote.

**Tab/focus:** enters FOCUS(domain). Use a clearly visible focus ring that is not colour-only. Expose exactly the same summary/status/CTA information available on pointer preview.

**Enter:** activates the focused canonical link/CTA.

**Space:** if the control is a button disclosure, toggles disclosure; if it is a normal link, preserve native link semantics rather than inventing button behavior.

**Escape:** closes an optional disclosure and returns to the domain trigger; it must never trap focus.

No custom arrow-key navigation is required unless the nodes are implemented with a composite ARIA widget. Prefer ordinary links/buttons and native keyboard behavior.

### Touch / coarse pointer

Hover is not required and must not be the only way to reveal information.

**Initial:** IDLE with every domain name and essential identity visible.

**First tap on a disclosure-style domain:** enters SELECTED(domain), revealing concise outcome, lifecycle cue and canonical CTA without navigation.

**Second action:** the explicit CTA navigates. Tapping another domain moves SELECTED to that domain.

**Dismiss:** tapping outside the disclosure or an explicit close control returns to IDLE. Do not make users discover a hidden gesture.

If a domain card is already self-explanatory and uses a direct link, a single tap may navigate; never require double-tap merely to follow a link.

No swipe/carousel dependency for the four primary domains.

### Mobile layout (target includes 390px)

At narrow widths, do not shrink the desktop constellation into an unreadable diagram.

Render:
1. locked eyebrow/headline/value/primary CTA;
2. a compact central 'governed KMJ ecosystem' visual anchor;
3. four vertically readable domain cards or a 2x2 layout when width permits;
4. lifecycle/status and canonical action inside the selected/expanded card.

The visual connection between domains may become a static line/grid motif rather than literal orbit geometry.

Touch targets should be at least 44x44 CSS px where practical. No horizontal scrolling is allowed. Essential product/domain text must not be truncated solely to preserve artwork.

Mobile transition duration: approximately 160–220ms. Prefer opacity, transform and grid/height techniques that avoid large layout shifts. Do not use long accordion animation for substantial content.

### Reduced motion

When `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` is active:

- disable orbiting, pulsing, parallax and continuous connection-energy movement;
- remove nonessential transform travel;
- use immediate state changes or a short <=100ms opacity transition;
- preserve every label, connection meaning, lifecycle cue and CTA;
- keep hover/focus/selection differentiation through border, surface, typography and static luminance;
- never autoplay video or animated canvas as a substitute.

Reduced motion is a complete first-class experience, not a degraded fallback.

### JavaScript unavailable / hydration failure

The server-rendered/Inertia-delivered hero must remain useful before interaction code runs:
- locked headline and value proposition visible;
- all four domain names visible;
- canonical links present as real anchors;
- lifecycle/status copy rendered from governed data where shown;
- no content hidden behind JS-only state.

CSS may provide progressive hover/focus enhancement. Navigation must not depend on animation state.

### Asset/network fallback

Hero meaning must not depend on successful loading of a decorative image.

For each critical visual:
- reserve dimensions/aspect ratio to prevent CLS;
- provide meaningful alt text when the image conveys product information, otherwise use decorative empty alt;
- use a designed gradient/surface fallback behind the asset;
- if an image fails, domain labels, outcome and CTA remain fully readable;
- never show broken-image UI as the primary hero focal point.

### Visibility and performance behavior

Continuous ambient animation pauses or becomes inert when the document is not visible. Avoid timers for semantic state. Do not preload every product artwork; preload only the genuinely critical hero asset(s).

Interaction must not trigger network requests merely from hover/focus. Prefetching, if used, must follow framework/browser policy and must not create excessive bandwidth use.

### Transition tokens

Use a small shared motion vocabulary rather than per-card animation inventions:
- micro feedback: 100–140ms;
- preview/focus reveal: 180–240ms;
- mobile disclosure: 160–220ms;
- section atmospheric handoff: 300–500ms and only when compositor-friendly;
- easing: a consistent ease-out for entering/emphasis and ease-in-out for ambient transforms.

Avoid bounce/spring effects for enterprise controls.

### Input-mode switching

Do not permanently infer input mode from viewport width. A touchscreen laptop may use keyboard, mouse and touch in one session.

Semantic state follows the latest explicit interaction:
- keyboard focus always wins while focused;
- pointer hover may preview only when hover capability exists;
- touch selection persists until explicit dismissal/another selection;
- focus-visible styling is preserved regardless of pointer history.

### Hero-to-next-section handoff

As the visitor scrolls naturally past the hero, the ecosystem connection motif may visually resolve into the first flagship/product section through a subtle background/grid/edge-light continuation. No scroll-jacking, pinned mandatory sequence or forced animation timeline.

The next section must be readable even if IntersectionObserver or animation enhancement is unavailable.

### Acceptance matrix

**Desktop mouse:** hover preview, stable CTA travel, click navigation, no auto-selection, no cursor hijack.

**Desktop keyboard:** logical tab order, visible focus, equivalent information, Enter/native activation, Escape only for disclosures, no focus trap.

**Touch:** no hover dependency, clear first-tap disclosure where used, explicit navigation action, simple dismissal.

**390px mobile:** no horizontal overflow, readable four-domain story, >=44px practical targets, no desktop diagram shrinkage.

**Reduced motion:** no continuous motion; all meaning and actions preserved.

**JS/asset failure:** headline, domain identity and real links remain usable; no blank hero.

A build is not accepted if any input mode receives materially less product meaning than another.

## 15. Signature hero — exact mobile touch choreography

### Mobile composition

At narrow/mobile widths, preserve the hero narrative in this order:
1. eyebrow;
2. locked headline and value proposition;
3. primary CTA;
4. compact governed-ecosystem anchor;
5. four domain cards: Cloud, Calibration, Immersive, AI + Remote.

Prefer a single-column card stack at the narrowest widths. A 2x2 arrangement is allowed only when labels, lifecycle cues and tap targets remain comfortably readable. Never reproduce the desktop orbit at a scale that makes targets or labels small.

### Tap targets

- Every interactive domain trigger must provide a practical minimum 44x44 CSS px target.
- Primary/secondary hero CTAs should target approximately 48px height.
- Keep at least 8px practical separation between independent adjacent tap targets.
- The entire clearly bounded domain-card header/summary area may act as the disclosure trigger.
- A navigation CTA inside an expanded card remains a separate explicit target.
- Decorative connection lines, glow, artwork and status ornaments are never tap targets.
- Do not place two different destinations behind overlapping hit areas.

### Initial state

On first render, all four domain names and a one-line identity/outcome are visible without interaction. No card is secretly preselected.

The central ecosystem anchor communicates connection/governance but is not an unexplained button. If it is interactive, its purpose and destination must be explicitly labelled.

### First tap — select/disclose

Tapping a disclosure-enabled domain card enters SELECTED(domain).

Selection feedback occurs immediately:
- border/surface emphasis changes;
- domain icon/title receives stronger contrast;
- connection motif to the ecosystem anchor may brighten;
- concise factual detail, lifecycle cue and canonical CTA become visible;
- other cards remain fully legible and tappable.

Do not navigate on this first disclosure tap. Do not require a long press.

### Selected card behavior

Only one domain disclosure is expanded at a time.

Tapping a different domain transfers selection directly to it; do not require closing the current card first.

Tapping the selected card's disclosure trigger again may collapse it to IDLE, but the explicit CTA must remain a normal navigation action and must never be overloaded as collapse.

Selection must not reset because of small incidental scroll movement.

### Navigation

Navigation occurs only through a clearly labelled canonical product/group CTA unless the card is intentionally designed as a direct-link card with no disclosure state.

Do not implement double-tap-to-navigate.

Opening links must preserve normal browser behavior. Do not intercept long-press link behavior, context menus or browser navigation gestures.

### Swipe and scroll behavior

The four primary domains must **not** depend on horizontal swipe, carousel pagination or drag gestures.

Vertical page scrolling always wins over decorative hero interaction.

- No custom vertical swipe interception.
- No `preventDefault` on ordinary touch scrolling.
- No scroll-jacking or mandatory snap sequence.
- No drag-to-orbit requirement.
- No edge-swipe interception; browser back/forward gestures remain untouched.

A decorative visual may react subtly after a completed selection, but finger movement itself must not continuously steer expensive animation.

### Outside tap / dismissal

If an expanded disclosure behaves like an overlay/popover, tapping outside dismisses it. Prefer inline expansion on mobile so outside-tap logic is unnecessary.

Scrolling into the next section may leave the selection state behind naturally; there is no requirement to animate a forced close.

Any explicit close affordance must have an accessible name and >=44x44 practical target.

### Selection feedback timing

Normal-motion path:
- touch-down/native press feedback: immediate;
- surface/border emphasis: 100–140ms;
- detail reveal: 160–220ms ease-out;
- connection highlight: may overlap detail reveal;
- collapse/transfer: <=200ms.

Avoid bounce, elastic springs, flashing, repeated pulse or large zoom. Maximum emphasis scale should be subtle (about 1.01) or omitted entirely on mobile.

Haptic feedback must not be required and should not be triggered by the website.

### Scroll-position stability

Expanding a card must not unexpectedly move the tapped trigger out from under the user's finger or jump the viewport.

Prefer reserved/controlled disclosure space or modest inline expansion. If content expansion would push substantial content, keep the selected card's heading visually anchored.

No automatic `scrollIntoView` on ordinary selection. It may be used only when necessary to recover an obscured focused control and must respect reduced motion.

### Orientation and resize

Do not persist geometry-dependent animation state across orientation changes. Preserve the semantic selected domain where practical, recompute layout, and render it in the new geometry.

No content may become inaccessible after portrait <-> landscape change.

### Reduced-motion mobile behavior

Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`:
- remove orbit, pulse, parallax, animated connection travel and scale emphasis;
- selection feedback becomes static border/surface/typography change;
- disclosure appears immediately or with <=100ms opacity;
- no animated auto-scroll;
- selected state, lifecycle cue, outcome and CTA remain identical in meaning;
- domain connection is represented with static lines/grid/grouping.

Reduced motion must not change navigation structure or require extra taps.

### Slow device / constrained network fallback

The touch experience must remain fully usable without decorative hero artwork.

Do not wait for large media before enabling domain controls. Render text, lifecycle and links first. Decorative imagery may progressively appear afterward without changing target positions.

Avoid loading all product artwork as a consequence of selecting one domain. A selection must not cause an unbounded network burst.

### Accessibility semantics

Use native buttons for disclosure triggers and native anchors for navigation.

For a disclosure button:
- expose `aria-expanded`;
- associate it with the disclosed region using `aria-controls` when appropriate;
- keep accessible name stable as state changes.

Do not announce decorative animation. Lifecycle text must be real readable text, not encoded only in badge colour.

### Mobile acceptance scenarios

**One-handed portrait at 390px:** visitor can read all four domains, select any domain and reach its CTA without horizontal scrolling.

**Fast repeated taps:** state resolves deterministically to the latest explicit domain; no stacked disclosures or queued animation cascade.

**Tap then scroll:** vertical scroll remains native and selection does not hijack the gesture.

**Touchscreen + keyboard:** keyboard focus semantics still work and visibly override purely visual touch emphasis while focused.

**Reduced motion:** same number of taps, same content and same destinations, with continuous/nonessential motion removed.

**Image failure/slow network:** cards and CTAs remain positioned, readable and actionable.

The mobile hero fails acceptance if understanding a primary domain requires a swipe, hover, long press, precision tap, animation completion or successful decorative-media download.
