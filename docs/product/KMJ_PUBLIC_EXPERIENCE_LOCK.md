# KMJ Public Experience — Locked Direction

**Status:** OWNER LOCKED  
**Scope:** KMJ Main Platform landing page, public product pages, product artwork, demos, SEO/AEO presentation and future public-facing additions.

This file is an authoritative product/design constraint. Future chats, developers, agents and automation must preserve it unless the owner explicitly changes the lock.

## 1. Experience objective

The public experience must feel premium, cinematic, modern, cooperative, professional and trustworthy. The first impression should be visually magnetic and memorable without becoming noisy, gimmicky, gaming-like or difficult to use.

Use strong visual hierarchy and intentional eye travel: focal imagery -> product meaning -> benefit -> proof/status -> next action.

## 2. Colour is semantic, not decoration

Do **not** make the whole site red.

- KMJ red: brand signature, primary actions and selective emphasis.
- Blue/cyan: cloud, connectivity, infrastructure and technical systems where meaningful.
- Green: verified/available/success states.
- Amber/orange: preview, early access, attention or transitional states.
- Neutral black/charcoal/white: enterprise foundation, readability and trust.
- Other colours may be used when they improve the meaning of a product, industry or scene.

Maintain visual cohesion through typography, spacing, surfaces, lighting and interaction language rather than forcing one accent colour everywhere.

## 3. Product artwork

Every public product must have its **own separate, unique product-specific artwork**. Do not use one combined collage as the product asset and do not substitute generic placeholder art.

Artwork should explain the product visually at a glance. Keep the premium cinematic quality while making products visibly distinct. In particular, KMJ 360 Dome and KMJ Cinema Player must never look like the same product.

Default artwork target: 4:3 master with intentional desktop/mobile crops. Optimize delivery for responsive loading and performance.

Do not place decorative KMJ/KMJ TECHNO logo watermarks over artwork. Product/device branding may appear only when it belongs naturally to the depicted product/interface.

## 4. Product communication

A customer should quickly understand:
1. What is this?
2. Who is it for?
3. What problem does it solve?
4. What concrete benefit does it provide?
5. What is its current lifecycle/availability?
6. Can I see or try it?
7. What is the appropriate next action?

Product copy should be persuasive, clear and commercially useful, but must remain factual. Never fabricate capabilities, customers, certifications, benchmarks, market leadership, availability or proof.

Do not publish unsupported claims such as "#1", "world's best", guaranteed results or guaranteed search rankings. The quality target may be world-class; public claims require evidence.

## 5. Interactive understanding

Where interaction materially helps understanding, provide a lightweight browser demo/simulation. Demos must be clearly labelled as simulations when they are not connected to a real backend/product environment.

Prioritize demos for products whose workflow or value is difficult to communicate with static copy alone. Demo UX must explain the workflow, not merely animate the page.

## 6. Search and AI discovery

Optimize each canonical product page for conventional search engines and AI answer/search systems through:
- unique intent-aligned titles and descriptions;
- crawlable factual product copy;
- clear headings and semantic page structure;
- Product/SoftwareApplication or other appropriate structured data where factually valid;
- FAQ content when it answers real customer questions;
- use cases and audience context;
- canonical URLs;
- strong internal linking from landing, portfolio and related products;
- descriptive image alt text and optimized media;
- consistent product names, lifecycle facts and terminology across surfaces.

Do not keyword-stuff, generate doorway pages, hide text or use deceptive SEO. Search ranking is an outcome, not a guaranteed claim.

## 7. Conversion without deception

Design the path from curiosity -> understanding -> confidence -> demo/trial/contact/purchase. Calls to action must match actual lifecycle and commercial readiness.

Purchase stays fail-closed unless governed product state allows it. Research/planned products must not be presented as currently purchasable. Early access, preview and trial labels must reflect actual platform state.

## 8. Performance and accessibility

Visual richness must not sacrifice speed, mobile usability, accessibility or stability. Use responsive media, lazy loading below the fold, appropriate eager/preload only for critical media, readable contrast, keyboard-accessible interactions and reduced-motion-friendly behavior.

Desktop and mobile are first-class experiences.

## 9. Landing-page role

The landing page should orchestrate the portfolio rather than repeat every product page. It must create a compelling first impression, surface flagship products, show the wider ecosystem, communicate industries/use cases and roadmap truth, and route users to canonical product pages.

Preserve the owner-locked hero copy and established brand identity unless explicitly changed by the owner.

## 10. Change-control rule

Before merging a public-experience change, verify:
- it follows this specification;
- product facts/lifecycle are truthful;
- artwork is product-specific;
- desktop and mobile rendering are healthy;
- no unexpected horizontal overflow or console/runtime errors;
- canonical product links work;
- relevant CI/tests pass;
- production is not deployed merely because a design change merged.

When visual preference conflicts with factual accuracy, security, accessibility, lifecycle governance or purchase controls, those safety/truth constraints win.
## 11. P0 world-class quality principle

This is a **P0 project priority**, not a cosmetic preference.

Every public-experience decision must be evaluated holistically across visual design, UX, product comprehension, factual accuracy, conversion, SEO/AEO discoverability, interactive demonstration, accessibility, responsive behavior, performance, security, maintainability and operational governance.

Do not optimize one dimension by damaging another. A visually impressive page that is slow, confusing, inaccessible, misleading or difficult to maintain does not meet the bar. Likewise, a technically correct page that feels generic, forgettable or commercially unclear does not meet the bar.

For each meaningful landing/product change, actively ask:
- Is the purpose immediately understandable?
- Is the visual hierarchy compelling and intentional from first glance through the next action?
- Are colour, imagery, motion and interaction meaningful rather than decorative noise?
- Does the experience feel mature, cooperative, professional and trustworthy?
- Are claims, lifecycle, pricing/access and demos truthful?
- Is the content useful to both humans and search/AI systems without keyword stuffing?
- Is there a clear conversion path appropriate to the product state?
- Does it work exceptionally well on mobile and desktop?
- Is it fast, accessible, secure and maintainable?
- Is this materially better than a generic template or ordinary product page?

The target is world-class execution quality. Never publish unsupported superiority claims merely to express that internal quality target.

## 12. Interactive product showroom standard

Treat the public platform as an interactive product showroom, not a conventional brochure website.

### Ten-second comprehension
Within roughly ten seconds on a product's first screen, a new visitor should be able to understand what the product is, who it serves, the primary problem/outcome, its current lifecycle state and the appropriate next action.

### Product-specific visual storytelling
Allow each product family to have meaningful visual atmosphere while preserving KMJ cohesion. Cloud/infrastructure may use cooler technical tones; calibration may use optical/precision cues; immersive cinema may use richer cinematic spectrum; security/licensing may use restrained enterprise cues. Colour must communicate, not merely decorate.

### Interactive demos as explanation
When a workflow benefits from interaction, make a lightweight interactive demo/simulation a primary explanation surface. Candidate experiences include calibration mapping/warping, CloudOS infrastructure control, remote-session flows, licensing activation/entitlements and cinema playback/show control. Simulations must be explicitly identified when they are not a real connected product environment.

### Contextual Kristi guidance
Kristi should behave as a contextual product guide rather than a generic chat bubble. On a product surface, guidance should be grounded in verified facts for that product and help visitors understand use cases, lifecycle, demos and appropriate next steps. Kristi must not invent capabilities, availability, pricing or proof.

### Evidence-first trust
Pair persuasive communication with verifiable product facts, lifecycle state, documentation/demo evidence and relevant security/architecture information. Do not fabricate customer counts, deployments, testimonials, awards, benchmarks, uptime/reliability percentages or other social proof.

### Intent-based discovery
In addition to product-name navigation, provide paths based on customer intent where useful: cloud infrastructure, immersive experiences, remote access, AI engineering, calibration, licensing and other evidence-backed solution groups.

### Search/AI entity architecture
Each product should have one canonical entity page with consistent naming and facts, appropriate structured data, crawlable use cases/FAQs, meaningful related-product links, sitemap discoverability and terminology consistent with the governed catalog.

### Performance budget
Cinematic presentation is subject to hard performance discipline. Prefer responsive AVIF/WebP where supported, correctly sized sources, critical-media prioritization, below-fold lazy loading, restrained animation and reduced-motion support. Core Web Vitals regressions must be treated as product defects, not accepted as a visual-design cost.

### Lifecycle-specific conversion
Calls to action must follow governed state:
- Available: eligible purchase/trial/demo/contact actions only when actually supported.
- Preview/Early Access/Public Beta: appropriate access/request/demo actions.
- Research/Planned: roadmap, learn-more or interest actions; never imply immediate purchase.
Purchase remains fail-closed under platform authority.

### Signature KMJ moment
Create a distinctive, performant hero/early-page interaction that communicates the connected KMJ ecosystem rather than looking like a generic template. A preferred concept is a controlled visual transition connecting Cloud -> Calibration -> Immersive Cinema -> AI/Remote systems as one governed ecosystem. It must remain usable, accessible and reduced-motion friendly.

### No fabricated proof
Concept artwork may contain illustrative visual placeholders during exploration, but production UI must not publish invented testimonials, customer identities, global deployment claims, reliability percentages, awards or numerical proof. Only evidence-backed proof may ship.

## 13. Signature KMJ ecosystem experience — implementation contract

### Hero interaction
Preserve the owner-locked headline: **Technology engineered to connect complex systems.**

The hero's signature interaction is a connected ecosystem constellation with four truthful domain nodes:
1. **Cloud** — infrastructure and CloudOS.
2. **Calibration** — precision mapping and Calibration Pro.
3. **Immersive** — cinema, dome and playback systems.
4. **AI + Remote** — engineering automation, secure remote/control products.

The interaction must communicate that these are distinct products/domains connected through the governed KMJ customer platform. It must not imply that unfinished products are generally available.

On capable pointers, focus/hover may emphasize one domain and reveal a concise factual outcome plus canonical product route. Keyboard focus must provide the same information. On touch/mobile, the nodes remain directly tappable and readable without requiring hover.

Use restrained CSS-first motion: slow ambient orbit/connection energy, small depth/parallax only where inexpensive, and short state transitions. Avoid scroll-jacking, autoplay video backgrounds, WebGL as a requirement, cursor hijacking, or continuous high-cost animation.

Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, render the complete ecosystem as a stable composition with no information loss.

### Eye-travel sequence
The intended visual reading path is:
**brand/domain eyebrow -> locked headline -> concise value proposition -> primary CTA -> ecosystem constellation -> proof/lifecycle cues -> next section**.

The visual focal point must never compete with the headline or CTA. Product meaning comes before spectacle.

### Landing narrative
The canonical section sequence is:
1. Signature hero / connected ecosystem.
2. Flagship product handoff.
3. Governed product ecosystem.
4. Immersive Cinema Suite.
5. Main Platform trust, licensing and controlled access.
6. Industries / customer-intent pathways backed by real product capabilities.
7. Truthful roadmap / lifecycle outlook.
8. Final conversion CTA and footer.

Sections may be combined when this improves mobile comprehension or performance, but the narrative order must remain recognizable.

### Section transitions
Transitions should feel like one continuous KMJ system rather than unrelated templates. Use shared grid/radius/typography/surface language plus semantic atmosphere shifts:
- Cloud/infrastructure: cool blue/cyan technical light.
- Calibration: precision optics, measured grids and controlled spectral cues.
- Immersive cinema: richer cinematic light and depth.
- AI/remote engineering: violet/cyan or other restrained computational cues.
- Licensing/security/control: neutral graphite with selective verified/status accents.
- KMJ red: brand signature and high-value action emphasis, not a blanket page colour.

Use gradients, edge light, subtle grid/line continuity and controlled background handoffs rather than hard decorative separators.

### Product handoff behavior
Each hero domain must lead to real canonical product destinations or a truthful grouped discovery surface. Never create dead visual controls. Lifecycle state remains visible when relevant, and CTA language must follow governed availability.

### Performance/accessibility gates
The signature experience must:
- remain understandable with CSS/JS motion disabled;
- preserve semantic headings and crawlable copy;
- support keyboard navigation and visible focus;
- avoid information encoded by colour alone;
- avoid horizontal overflow at 390px;
- avoid layout shift from hero media;
- keep critical hero media bounded and responsive;
- introduce no console/runtime errors;
- treat Core Web Vitals regressions as defects.

### Acceptance test
The experience passes only if a first-time visitor can answer, without scrolling deeply:
- What does KMJ TECHNO build?
- What are the major solution domains?
- How are they connected?
- Which product/path should I explore next?
- What is actually available versus preview/research/planned?

Visual magnetism is required, but comprehension, truth, performance and professional trust are hard constraints.
